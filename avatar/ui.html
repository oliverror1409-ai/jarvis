    THE NETWORK, EXPLAINED

    Every dot is one real process on this PC right now, sized by its memory. Colour is its family — each program owns one wedge of the ring, quiet grey is Windows itself. Lines are real parent-and-child links; a curved fan is a program holding its children. The bright cyan lines are two programs genuinely talking over an open socket — the rarest fact on the glass. Moving sparks are actual bytes: reads flow out to children, writes and CPU flow back up. A glowing dot is burning CPU right now, and the busiest carry name chips. The core is Jarvis; nothing may cross it.
